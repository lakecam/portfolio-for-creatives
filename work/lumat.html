Lumat

Turning spreadsheets into branded charts

A tool for designing branded and accurate charts in a lightweight package that completely runs on your Mac

Problem

Plots without proof or
personality

Chart tools promise interactive, data-fed charts without the technical overhead, but often skip checking and struggle with messy spreadsheets, causing fundamental issues that taint the integrity of the data while presenting it as finalized. Business Intelligence platforms keep precision, but maintain a steep learning curve.

After all that, the output is often generic, with paid plans unlocking superficial changes like colour and logo, while the underlying visual is still indistinguishable in different brands, leaving users with only general design tools which don’t dynamically alter the charts as new data is uploaded, forcing a re-design every time.

Audience

Experts in their field,
newer to data

Practitioners who need charts that look like their own organization’s, despite data visualization being outside their expertise. Product designers, entrepreneurs, researchers, nonprofit executives, and others who share metrics with stakeholders, yet have to resort to workarounds to complete their tasks correctly.

Research

Messy files created a mirage that hid all qualms

Tests were conducted by uploading disorganized spreadsheets into 5 high-profile products that compete in similar problem spaces. While some created clean, albeit templated, visualizations, they all produced charts that looked right, yet showed wrong or nonsensical numbers.

Pivots

What changed after research

Initially scoped around helping nonprofits decide which impact metrics to track, the tool’s focus shifted after interviews with 4 program managers of varying experience, all describing impact data that had to be rebuilt for each funder instead of sent as one template, and Excel charts that could show the numbers, yet never in their organization’s own colours.

Further research showed this bottleneck extended well past nonprofits, particularly to sectors where funding increases hinged on proving success via data, which broadened the product’s audience instead of keeping it to a single sector. Default grey charts make every organisation look the same, whereas a visual that people can read at a glance is more likely to win buy-in.

Design system

Change the brand once, every chart follows

Colours are named by role, such as text, warning or brand, so updating the brand kit in a central place applies automatically across all buttons, labels and charts.

Looking back

Bad charts start with bad data

What's known

Across 11 runs by one tester, none of the 4 tools checked gave a warning and none of the 5 explained its chart choice. Only the earlier Brite research involved real people.

Still assumed

It’s still an assumption that designers feel this pain, will read written warnings and lean on explanations when they pick a chart.

Test plan

Test 3 designers in Lumat and Datawrapper with the messy food-bank file. Count errors caught and time to export, then redesign or cut warnings and reasons that go unused.

Reflection

Datawrapper’s data checks and Flourish’s chart suggestions showed what Lumat needs to do: point out errors, explain each chart choice and block misleading charts. The findings come from testing tools, so they don’t yet show how people would use Lumat.

UX strategy

Show proof and materials up front

Considering junior urban planners are often the first line of vetting a vendor, it was ideal for our digital platforms to articulate Spriig’s technical depth, yet also feel fresh, approachable, and tactile, instead of the sterility of many competitors.

Decisions

Three decisions, each tied to a problem

Decision 01

Past builds as case studies

Instead of a one-size-fits-all catalogue, Projects shows each build’s location, date, and size, giving planners past work to point to. Browsing is slower as a result.

Decision 02

The material, in person

Planners find vendors at trade shows, so the booth brings real playground parts and a recycling wall, instead of banners alone.

Decision 03

Themed playgrounds

Themes add depth and story, instead of abstract forms. The UFO was picked for being fun and easy to identify.

Brand system · led by Kayla Do

Bright colour, grounded in texture

The mix of the digital and physical was key, with Kayla Do's palette paired with fabric, paper, and wood grain, for a voice that is imaginative, yet also structured and grounded.

Website

Every page leads to proof, then one contact

Spriig needed a friendly, informative website to showcase projects and partners, attract clients, and report on sustainability, with graphic shapes guiding people to content and a call to action closing every page.

Trade show booth

“Yes! These are chopsticks.”

Since trade shows matter so much for playground equipment, the booth needed a playful, memorable presence, with real playground parts on display and a wall explaining how chopsticks are recycled.

Playground concept

A UFO with its safety case built in

Following a narrative approach, the playground takes a UFO theme, with a ramp instead of stairs for accessibility, and no fully enclosed walls, for safety.

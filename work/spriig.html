Decisions

Three decisions, each tied to a problem

Decision 01 · Answers 01, 03

Case studies, not a catalogue

Case studies show each project’s location, date and size, because the planner pointed out that approved lists don’t guarantee good work. Browsing is slower than with a catalogue.

Decision 02 · Answers 02

Let the material sell itself

Planners find vendors at trade shows, so the booth brings real playground parts and a recycling wall instead of relying on banners, at the cost of heavier freight.

Decision 03 · Answers 01

An explainable theme

The UFO was picked over Kayla Do’s other themes because its ramp and open walls give council an easy safety argument. The downside is that the whole booth tells one story.

Trade show booth

“Yes! These are chopsticks.”

The banner proclaims the unconventional material, a UFO section invites touch, and the wall shows 7 recycling steps. The slide and ladder use an estimated 20,000 chopsticks, scaled from ChopValue’s recycled-chopstick yoga blocks.

Playground concept

A UFO with its safety case built in

The UFO gives Amy, the parent persona, the tactile play she wants, and its ramp and open walls make it accessible, transparent, and safe.

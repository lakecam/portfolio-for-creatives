Spriig

Play structures from recycled chopsticks

A website, 3D models and a trade show booth for a B Corp studio that builds play structures from 100% recycled chopsticks.

Role

UX/UI, wireframing, 3D modelling

Timeline

Spring 2026

Team

With Kayla Do (branding)

Deliverables

Website, 3D models, booth

Audience

Junior urban planners at cities, park boards and school districts, who often shortlist the vendor, and the families who play on what gets built.

Problem

Used chopsticks as a building material

  • A B Corp studio

    Spriig is a B Corp-certified social enterprise and industrial design studio that builds play structures from 100% recycled chopsticks, made for families.

  • Slow manufacturing

    Its process prefers local services and takes the time to find affordable, low-carbon ways to make products, instead of unethical cost-cutting.

  • Profit, without cutting corners

    The primary goal was profit while staying ethical in manufacturing, distribution and marketing, with locally produced products, community impact, transparency and ongoing education.

  • The question

    Disposable wooden utensils, like chopsticks, are a rich raw material source, so how can they be repurposed for everyday city use?

UX Strategy

Technical depth that still feels fresh

Considering Spriig's target clients, especially junior urban planners who are often the first line of vetting in procuring a vendor, it was ideal for the digital platforms to articulate the technical depth of its expertise, yet also feel fresh, approachable, and tactile, instead of the sterility of many competitors.

The sitemap stays small, with Home, About, Learn, Projects and Contact.

Concept

Themed playgrounds tell a story

Owing to the research, and the UX strategy, the concept developed into a multi-touchpoint design that includes both the website and social media, and physical, experiential pieces such as a trade show booth. Each playground embodies a theme based on the client's interests or locale, inspiring creativity in kids instead of the abstract ideas of most playgrounds.

Visual identity

Bright colour, grounded in texture

The mix of the digital and physical was key, with the colour palette being paired with fabric, paper, and wood grain textures, for that final push towards a brand voice that is imaginative and creative, yet also structured and grounded.

Playground concept

A UFO you can walk into

A UFO theme was chosen for today's era of deep space exploration, but mostly because it's fun and easy to identify with. For accessibility it has a ramp instead of stairs, and for public safety there are no fully enclosed walls.

Trade show booth

“Yes! These are chopsticks.”

Considering trade shows are hugely important for playground equipment, it was ideal for Spriig to have a standout presence that is memorable, playful, and friendly, with key parts of the actual playground in front of visitors, and a full-wall infographic showing the chopstick recycling process.

Web design

Friendly, modern, informative

Spriig needed a website that showcases its projects and partners, attracts new customers, and reports on its sustainability work. Graphic shapes guide people to content, and every page ends with a call to action once the content has been read.

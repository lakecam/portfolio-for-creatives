Poba

Making friends via board games

An app that turns board game nights into real friendships. Players find or host a night nearby, and after each one, those who want to play again become a regular group.

First Idea, 2025

First: match by personality

Zari, the 2025 version, used Myers-Briggs (MBTI) to avoid a night at the wrong table. It answered who to meet—not what happens after.

Pivot

Interviews ruled out personality matching

4 interviewees saw personality matching as a gimmick. A 2025 review also flagged intrusive questions and data-security risks. The group became the product, not a better match.

Finding

In 4 interviews, personality matching read as a gimmick rather than a real need.

Change

Groups now form around how people like to play, and 1 question after each night keeps good groups together.

Iteration

Rating the night, not the people

Rating each player felt like ranking people, which hurt quieter players most, so everyone answers 1 question instead.

Decision

Home leads with the next night

Free events lose around a third of sign-ups. Home therefore leads with the next night and Check in, not a most-played games ranking.

Final screens

Every screen leads back to the same table

12 screens follow one player towards a second night, grouped by the problem they answer. Sign-up starts with a real night so newcomers see what they are joining.

Look and feel

From Greek to nostalgic play

Poba started as Zari, Greek for dice, so the first look drew on ancient Greek art. However, this felt contrived, so a brighter look based on '90s party games replaced it to tap into nostalgia instead

Status

Designed, not yet tested

4 interviews and desk research support dropping personality matching. Play-style grouping and “Run it back?” remain assumptions until people book a second night. No sessions yet.

Guided prototype sessions

Who: 3 groups of 4 to 8 players
Measure: "Run it back?" yeses, then date votes
Changes the design: if most answer no, rethink grouping; if few vote, simplify dates

Hand-run game nights

Who: 10 to 15 players, one Vancouver neighbourhood
Measure: return to the same table within 30 days
Changes the design: below 7%, the share of iOS dating-app users still active at day 30, add more between nights
